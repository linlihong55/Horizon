## [Anthropic says Claude discovered a novel enzyme system with CRISPR-like repeats](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-discovers-novel-enzyme-system) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Anthropic announced that its Claude AI model discovered a previously undescribed enzyme system in the DNA of bacteriophages, where a gene encoding a reverse transcriptase sits next to a long tandem repeat array resembling a CRISPR array. The company published the finding in a news post, highlighting the agent&\#x27;s own transcript exclamation about spotting the repeat array &\#x27;by eye&\#x27;. The claim is a high-profile example of an AI agent being credited with a genuine scientific discovery rather than just assisting human researchers, which could reshape how genomics labs use LLMs to scan raw sequence data. It also fuels an ongoing industry debate over whether such AI-driven findings represent a paradigm shift in science or just automated pattern-spotting on known biological components. The system appears to involve a known retron-like reverse transcriptase, and skeptics argue the novelty lies mainly in a previously undescribed genomic arrangement rather than a brand-new enzyme. The discovery was made from raw phage DNA sequence data, and the CRISPR-like repeats are noteworthy because natural CRISPR arrays normally consist of short repeats separated by spacers, which is what enables their adaptive immunity function.

hackernews · raahelb · Sep 23, 18:06 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49820134)

**Background**: CRISPR is a bacterial immune system whose array is built from short repeated DNA sequences separated by spacers, and it has been repurposed for gene editing. Reverse transcriptases are enzymes that copy RNA back into DNA, and they appear in diverse mobile genetic elements such as retrons. Bacteriophages are viruses that infect bacteria, and their genomes are a common source of newly characterized defense and anti-defense systems.

## [ShinyHunters claims FBI breach, says it holds data on all employees](https://www.404media.co/we-hacked-the-fbi-hackers-say-they-have-data-on-all-fbi-employees/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The extortion group ShinyHunters claims it breached multiple FBI-related services and stole data covering all FBI employees and job applicants, according to a 404 Media report. The group provided a sample containing roughly 5,000 alleged FBI employees&\#x27; records, which appear to include names, home addresses, phone numbers and information about family members such as spouses; the FBI has not confirmed the claim. If the data is authentic, the leaked personal information could be used to track, harass or threaten FBI employees and their families, and it could represent a serious security and counterintelligence risk to U.S. law enforcement and intelligence systems. The claim also highlights how extortion-focused cybercrime groups continue to target high-profile government-adjacent organizations. The publicly shown evidence is only a sample of about 5,000 records, far smaller than the claim of covering every FBI employee and applicant, and the data&\#x27;s authenticity has not been independently verified. Because the FBI has neither confirmed nor denied the breach, the true scope and the method of intrusion remain unknown.

**Background**: ShinyHunters is a black-hat hacking and extortion group that has been active since around 2019-2020 and has been linked to a large number of data breaches. It typically gains access through cloud misconfigurations, OAuth token theft via integration vendors, supply-chain attacks, zero-day exploits and voice phishing or other social engineering, then exfiltrates data and demands a ransom, leaking or selling the stolen information on the dark web if the victim refuses to pay. The group is believed to be affiliated with The Com, a large international network of cybercriminals.
