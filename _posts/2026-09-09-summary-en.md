## [Terence Tao Warns AI May Deplete Open Problems and Erode Open Science](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Sep/9/terence-tao/) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Terence Tao, a prominent mathematician, posted on Mathstodon that AI-powered efforts are mining open problems in a non-renewable fashion. He warns that even rumors of someone working on a problem can now trigger mass AI effort, discouraging researchers from sharing promising directions. This matters because sharing research directions is a centuries-old tradition in open science; if incentives shift toward secrecy, the long-term health of mathematics and other fields could be seriously damaged. It also highlights a novel societal risk of AI: changing community incentives rather than merely solving individual problems. Tao’s exact concern is that public ideas may be &\#x27;flattened&\#x27; by automated theorem-proving efforts before the original researcher can develop them fully. The commentary was shared on Mathstodon and highlighted by Simon Willison under tags including AI ethics, mathematics, and AI.

rss · Simon Willison · Sep 9, 00:20

**Background**: In mathematics, &\#x27;open problems&\#x27; are unsolved questions considered important and fruitful for guiding future research. Traditionally, openly sharing such problems helps the whole community collaborate and build on each other&\#x27;s ideas. As AI systems become capable of exploring and proving theorems at scale, the incentive structure may change: researchers who broadcast a direction fear that automated agents will quickly finish it, so they may keep ideas secret. Tao&\#x27;s &\#x27;non-renewable mining&\#x27; metaphor suggests that once a good problem is solved, it is gone for good, and the accelerating pace of AI could deplete the supply of fruitful open problems.

## [OpenAI Claims Unverified Navier–Stokes Solution, Sparking Priority Dispute](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Sep/8/on-navier-stokes/) ⭐️ 9.0/10

OpenAI announced in September 2026 that it used an unreleased internal model to produce a proposed solution to the Navier–Stokes existence and smoothness problem, one of the Clay Mathematics Institute&\#x27;s Millennium Prize Problems, and said the argument was formalized in Lean. The company claims the result establishes statements C and D of the official problem formulation, but it has not yet been independently verified or assessed by the Clay Institute. If verified, this would be the first solution to a Millennium Prize Problem produced by an AI system and a dramatic demonstration of AI reasoning ability. The accompanying priority dispute raises important questions about research ethics, data access, and authorship when AI assistants and rival labs work near-simultaneously on the same open problem. OpenAI says its agents sent 4.9 million messages and used roughly 300 billion output tokens across all attempted problems, a compute cost of about $15 million if billed at public GPT-6 Astra API prices. **Background**: The Navier–Stokes equations describe the motion of fluids. The existence and smoothness problem for the three-dimensional case asks whether smooth, globally defined solutions always exist for reasonable initial conditions, or whether a counterexample can be found. In May 2000 the Clay Mathematics Institute selected this problem as one of its seven Millennium Prize Problems, each carrying a US$1,000,000 prize; only the Poincaré conjecture has been officially solved so far. According to available accounts, OpenAI&\#x27;s claimed result would be a finite-time singularity counterexample rather than a proof of global smoothness, but it has not been peer-reviewed by the mathematical community.

## [Qwen3.8 27B Quantization Benchmarks: 4-bit Holds Up, 1-bit Collapses](https://quesma.com/blog/qwen38-27b-quantizations-benchmarked/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A Quesma benchmark of GGUF quantization levels for Qwen3.8 27B shows that 4-bit variants hold up well, while 1-bit quantization significantly degrades output quality. Quantization is essential for deploying large language models on consumer and enterprise GPUs, since it reduces memory usage and compute costs. This result gives practitioners practical evidence that 4-bit quantized files are a reliable choice, while 1-bit formats are not yet usable for quality-sensitive tasks. According to the chart discussion, quality remains close to the unquantized model down to 4-bit, while 2-bit scores somewhat lower and 1-bit collapses. The benchmark does not yet cover KV-cache quantization, which commenters identify as an important variable for long-context usage, such as fitting about 100k tokens inside 24GB of VRAM.

hackernews · stared · Sep 8, 14:49 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49611128)

**Background**: Quantization lowers the numerical precision of model weights, for example from 32-bit floats to values stored at 4 bits per weight, cutting memory usage and speeding up inference while ideally keeping accuracy close to the original. In local LLM deployment, GGUF formats such as Q4\_K\_M are widely used through runtimes like llama.cpp and Ollama. Qwen3.8 27B is an open-weight, dense vision-language model suited for coding, professional workflows, and agent tasks, so reproducible quantization benchmarks are valuable for people choosing how to run it on limited hardware.
