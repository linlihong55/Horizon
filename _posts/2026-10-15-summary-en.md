## [First Stargardt patient receives PRIMA visual chip, reads a word](https://science.xyz/news/stargardt-first-rehab/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Science Corporation implanted its PRIMA visual restoration chip into a patient with Stargardt disease; the surgery took place in May at the Sydney Eye Hospital in Australia, and CEO Max Hodak reported that during the first rehabilitation session the patient could see letters and by the second session could read out a word. This is the first time a Stargardt disease patient has been implanted with the PRIMA chip, marking an early but notable step toward restoring form vision in a currently incurable cause of blindness; if the clinical trial holds up, subretinal photovoltaic implants could become a treatment pathway for the roughly 150,000 people worldwide blinded by the disease, and more broadly for retinal degenerative conditions. The procedure was performed at the Sydney Eye Hospital and the results come from early clinical testing rather than a completed trial, so the outcome for a single patient is preliminary; PRIMA has already received CE marking in Europe for use in age-related macular degeneration \(where it is described as the first BCI device cleared for form vision restoration\), while the Stargardt implantation is being carried out under a clinical trial.

**Background**: Stargardt disease is a rare inherited form of juvenile macular degeneration in which fatty deposits build up on the macula, the central part of the retina, causing progressive loss of central vision; the light-sensing photoreceptors in the outer retina degenerate while the inner retinal neurons that carry signals to the brain largely survive. PRIMA is a subretinal photovoltaic implant: a tiny chip placed under the retina works together with a special pair of glasses that projects pulsed near-infrared light onto it, and each pixel converts that light into a local electrical current that stimulates the remaining inner retinal neurons, effectively replacing the lost photoreceptors.
