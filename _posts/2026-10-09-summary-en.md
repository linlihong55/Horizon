## [SpaceX to Acquire Nationwide Low-Band Spectrum for Starlink Mobile](https://x.com/SpaceX/status/2108291133025698301) ⭐️ 8.0/10

SpaceX announced an agreement to acquire a portfolio of nationwide low-band spectrum licenses in the United States, positioning Starlink Mobile to become a major US mobile broadband provider. The company says that combining this spectrum with its Gen2 constellation will let Starlink Mobile deliver high-speed mobile broadband to Americans no matter where they are. If completed, the deal would push SpaceX well beyond satellite internet and into direct competition with established US carriers such as Verizon, AT&amp;T and T-Mobile. It also signals a convergence of satellite and terrestrial networks, potentially extending coverage into rural and remote areas that terrestrial operators struggle to serve profitably. Low-band spectrum \(generally below 3 GHz\) is the classic &quot;coverage layer&quot;: signals travel farther and penetrate buildings better than mid- or high-band airwaves, but they carry less capacity and lower peak speeds. SpaceX did not disclose the seller, purchase price, or regulatory approval timeline in this announcement, and its Gen2 constellation is planned to contain almost 30,000 satellites, about two-thirds of them in very low Earth orbit below 450 km.

**Background**: Mobile carriers rely on licensed radio spectrum, granted by regulators such as the FCC in the US, to operate cellular networks; different frequency bands trade off coverage against capacity, which is why operators combine low, mid and high bands. Starlink is SpaceX&\#x27;s satellite internet service, and Starlink Mobile is its effort to offer direct connectivity to phones, traditionally by partnering with terrestrial carriers for roaming. Owning nationwide low-band licenses would let SpaceX use spectrum it controls rather than depending on partners, which is why this step is being framed as a move toward becoming a full mobile carrier.
